A good commercial cleaning quote starts with good information. When a provider understands your building, your schedule, and what you expect, the price you receive is more likely to match the work you actually need. When the request is vague, quotes come back hard to compare, and surprises tend to show up after service begins.

This guide explains how to prepare a commercial cleaning quote request in San Angelo, what details to gather, what to expect during a walkthrough, and how to review the proposal you receive. ## Know what you are asking for

Before you contact anyone, decide what kind of service you want priced. Most requests fall into one of a few groups:

* Recurring cleaning on a set schedule, such as nightly, a few times a week, or weekly.
* A one time project, such as a deep clean, a move out clean, or cleanup after construction or renovation.
* Periodic work added to routine service, such as carpet extraction or floor finish maintenance.
* Coverage for a short period, such as an event, a busy season, or a staffing gap.

If you need more than one of these, say so in the same request. A provider can often schedule periodic work around recurring visits, and listing everything at once helps you see the full picture before you sign anything.

## Describe your building

The size and layout of a building shape the workload more than almost anything else. You do not need architectural drawings, but a short written description goes a long way.

Include these basics:

* The building address and the type of facility, such as an office, school, church, retail store, or warehouse office.
* The approximate square footage that will be cleaned, and how many floors.
* The number of restrooms, break rooms, kitchens, and public entrances.
* The main floor types, such as carpet, tile, vinyl, or sealed concrete.
* Areas that should be skipped or that require supervised access, such as server rooms, records rooms, or offices with sensitive files.

If you are not sure of the square footage, a lease, a property record, or a facilities contact may have it. A rough estimate with a note that it is approximate is better than leaving it out, and the walkthrough can confirm it.

## Explain how the building is used

Two buildings with the same square footage can need very different cleaning. Traffic, hours, and the kind of work done inside all change what a crew has to do.

Tell the provider when the building is open, how many people use it on a typical day, and whether the public comes in. Mention busy seasons, regular events, or weekends when the space is used heavily. For a school, note the academic calendar and any summer or holiday periods when deeper work could be done. For a church or event hall, note when services or bookings usually happen.

Also mention local conditions you have noticed. Many West Texas buildings deal with dust tracked in at entrances, and offices near a parking lot or open ground may need more frequent attention at doors and on hard floors.

## List the tasks and how often

A written task list keeps every quote tied to the same expectations. Group tasks by area and frequency so a provider can see exactly what is included.

For example, restrooms might need daily cleaning of fixtures, mirrors, and floors, plus restocking. Offices might need trash removal and vacuuming several times a week, with dusting on a weekly rotation. Break rooms might need counters, sinks, and appliance exteriors wiped on every visit. Periodic tasks, such as high dusting, baseboards, or carpet extraction, should be listed with how often you expect them.

## Decide who supplies what

Supplies are a common source of confusion in commercial cleaning. Decide ahead of time whether your business or the provider will buy restroom paper, hand soap, trash liners, and cleaning products. If you have a preference for certain products, or if your building has existing dispensers that need specific refills, say so in the request.

Ask each provider to state clearly in the proposal which supplies are included and which are not. That one line can explain a large part of the difference between two quotes.

## Share access and schedule details

A provider needs to know when and how the crew can get into the building. Tell them whether you want cleaning during business hours, after hours, or early in the morning. Explain how entry works, whether that is keys, badges, door codes, or an alarm system, and whether anyone from your staff needs to be present.

If you need service to start by a certain date, include that date in the request. A clear start date helps the provider tell you honestly whether they can meet it. ## Gather a few photos

Photos are helpful before a walkthrough and are especially useful for one time projects. Take clear pictures of restrooms, main floors, entrances, break rooms, and any problem areas, such as stained carpet, floor buildup along the edges, or dusty vents. Photos of an area after a busy day show the real condition better than pictures taken right after cleaning.

Photos do not replace a walkthrough, but they help a provider prepare good questions and bring the right information to the visit.

## Expect a walkthrough

For most commercial buildings, a reliable quote requires a walkthrough. During the visit, the provider looks at the actual layout, the condition of floors and restrooms, the access points, and the details that photos and descriptions miss.

Use the walkthrough to share your priorities. Point out what bothers you most, such as restrooms that fall behind or entrances that never look finished. A careful provider will ask questions, take notes, and confirm what is in scope rather than guessing. Pay attention to how they listen, because that often reflects how they will handle concerns later.

## Review the proposal carefully

When the proposal arrives, check that it matches what you asked for. A clear proposal should describe the areas covered, the tasks included, how often each task happens, the service days and times, and who supplies consumables. It should also explain how one time work, such as an initial deep clean, is priced separately from recurring service.

If you are comparing more than one provider, line up the scopes side by side before you look at price. A lower number that leaves out restrooms on certain days or skips periodic floor care is not the same service.
