If you manage cleaning for more than one building, you already know the problem. The main office has a detailed task list from years ago. The branch office across town has a note taped inside the supply closet. The field office out of town has whatever the last provider happened to do. When something goes wrong, nobody can say what was supposed to happen, because each site is running on a different agreement.

This guide is for office managers, operations leads, and owners who handle cleaning for two or more locations in San Angelo and surrounding West Texas. It shows how to write one scope that covers every site without pretending every site is the same, so you can request comparable quotes, hold a provider to the work, and add or drop a location later without starting over. Master Commercial Clean cleans offices, schools, and commercial buildings across this region, and the structure below is how we would want a multi site request to reach us.

## Start with one master scope and one sheet per site

The most useful structure has two layers. The master scope holds everything that is true at every location. Each site sheet holds everything that is specific to one building.

The master scope usually covers:

- The standard task list for offices, restrooms, break rooms, and entrances.
- Your quality expectations and how problems get reported.
- Who supplies paper, soap, liners, and cleaning products.
- Insurance, background, and access requirements that apply everywhere.
- Invoicing, payment terms, and contract length.

Each site sheet then lists the address, approximate size, room list, floor types, visit days, work window, access method, and anything unusual about that building. A site sheet should fit on one page. If it runs longer, some of that material probably belongs in the master scope.

This split keeps shared rules from drifting apart. Change the restroom standard once, and update one sheet when a site adds a break room.

## Write site sheets from a walkthrough, not from memory

Managers of several buildings tend to describe the one they visit most and assume the rest match. They rarely do. A branch with a public lobby and a warehouse office with heavy tracked dirt need different time even at similar square footage.

Walk each site before you write its sheet, or have the person who works there walk it with you. Count restrooms and fixtures, note floor materials, find the dumpster, and check where supplies are stored. Our [commercial building cleaning walkthrough checklist for property managers](/guides/commercial-building-cleaning-walkthrough-checklist-property-managers) covers what to record in each room.

Note the hardest week, not just the normal one. A site that hosts monthly trainings, sees muddy boots after rain, or gets busy at the end of a quarter needs that written down. At Master Commercial Clean, we price each building for a full crew on its hardest night rather than its easiest one, so a site sheet that names the busy periods helps any provider quote honestly.

## Decide what must be identical and what can vary

Some things should be the same everywhere. Restroom standards, trash handling, how a reported problem gets answered, and who holds keys are good candidates. Consistency here makes inspections simple and gives your staff one set of expectations.

Other things should follow the building. Visit frequency is the clearest example. A busy main office might need service five evenings a week while a small satellite office does well with two. Forcing the same frequency on every site either overpays for the quiet ones or underserves the busy one.

Mark each line in the master scope as either fixed for all sites or set by the site sheet. That one habit prevents most arguments later, because everyone can see which rules are firm and which were chosen for a particular building.

## Plan access and security site by site

Access is where multi site service most often stalls before it starts. Each building may use different keys, alarm codes, gate systems, or badge requirements. Secured sites sometimes require every crew member to hold a badge before the first visit, and badging can take time.

For each site sheet, record:

- How the crew gets in and who issues keys or codes.
- Alarm procedures and who to call if it goes off.
- Areas that are locked, restricted, or off limits.
- Any badge, escort, or sign in requirement.
- An onsite contact for that building, if there is one.

Ask the provider to confirm how they will track keys and codes across all locations and how they handle a lost key. Our guide to [replacing your janitorial company without a gap in service](/guides/replace-janitorial-company-without-service-gap) covers key and code handoff in more detail.

## Get insurance and paperwork right for each owner

When all of your sites belong to one company, one set of vendor paperwork may cover them. When the buildings are leased from different landlords, or belong to separate entities, each one may have its own requirements.

Common requests include an IRS Form W-9, a certificate of insurance, and specific certificate holder or additional insured wording. List those needs on each site sheet so the provider can gather them before work begins. When a customer asks for a particular certificate holder or additional insured wording, we request an updated certificate from our insurer with that exact wording rather than sending a generic one, and you should expect the same care from any provider you hire.

## Set invoicing and sales tax up front

Decide early whether you want one invoice for all locations or a separate invoice for each site. Separate invoices help when locations have different budgets, cost centers, or owners. A single invoice is simpler when one office pays for everything. Either way, ask for each site's charge to appear on its own line so you can see what each building costs.

Sales tax deserves its own conversation. The Texas Comptroller treats janitorial and custodial services as taxable and says providers should collect state tax plus any applicable local taxes on the amount billed. See the Comptroller's publication on [Cleaning and Janitorial Services](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/94-111.php). Local taxes depend on where the work happens, so ask how each site's tax will be figured. If an entity that owns one of your locations holds a Texas sales tax exemption, send the certificate for that entity before the first invoice. When a customer provides an exemption certificate, we bill that account without sales tax.

Master Commercial Clean sends invoices at the start of each month. Whatever schedule you agree on, put it and the payment terms in the master scope so every site is billed the same way.

## Name one contact and a review rhythm

With several buildings, problems can bounce between people. Pick one person on your side who receives reports from every site and one provider contact who answers them. Each site can still have a local contact who notices issues, but the reports should flow to the same place.

Agree on a review rhythm. A short check at each site during the first weeks, then a regular review after that, catches scope gaps while they are easy to fix. Keep a simple log of each issue, the site, the date, and how it was resolved. Patterns across sites tell you whether the problem is one building or the scope itself.

## Know what a single provider can and cannot cover

One provider for every location is convenient, but only if they actually perform the work you need at each site. Ask directly which tasks their own crew will do and which they would hire out. Specialty work such as floor finish maintenance often comes from a different company. Master Commercial Clean does not offer flooring services, so if your sites need floor finish work, plan for a separate floor specialist alongside our recurring cleaning.

Also ask how the provider will cover a site when a scheduled crew member is out,, and how far each location is from their crews, since distance affects how quickly someone can fix a missed task.

## Use the scope to request comparable quotes

Once the master scope and site sheets are ready, send the same package to every provider you are considering. Ask each one to price every site separately and to state the recurring price, any project work, and supplies on their own lines. That turns several building quotes into one comparison you can actually read.
